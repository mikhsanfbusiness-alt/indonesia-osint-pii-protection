# Project Overview

## Indonesia OSINT & PII Protection Framework

### 1. Purpose

The Indonesia OSINT & PII Protection Framework is a codeless, documentation-first cybersecurity project. It helps learners and organizations understand personal-data exposure risks in the Indonesian context, prioritize protection, and document defensive assessments in a consistent way.

The project responds to recurring concerns about data exposure, identity fraud, phishing, account compromise, and the misuse of personal information. Its purpose is prevention and responsible risk assessment, not the discovery, collection, or redistribution of private records.

### 2. Objectives

- Describe common personal-data categories relevant to Indonesia.
- Provide a proposed, risk-based ranking to help prioritize protective controls.
- Offer repeatable checklists for authorized organizational assessments and defensive OSINT.
- Provide templates for inventories, evidence logs, risk assessments, and incident reports.
- Demonstrate the framework with synthetic or carefully anonymized examples.
- Encourage privacy-aware handling and responsible reporting of suspected exposure.

### 3. Scope

The framework covers:

- Personal identifiers such as NIK/KTP references, family-card information, tax identifiers, contact details, and vehicle-related records.
- Sensitive data categories such as health, biometric, child-related, and personal financial information.
- Digital identity and security secrets, including passwords, session tokens, API keys, and private keys. These are included because compromise can create direct security risk; their legal classification may differ from personal-data categories.
- Organizational data inventories, exposure assessment, defensive OSINT, risk ranking, mitigation planning, and incident documentation.
- Indonesian legal and institutional references, which users should verify before relying on them for operational or compliance decisions.

### 4. Out of scope

This project does not provide or endorse:

- Searching for, acquiring, or publishing real people's NIK, KK, NPWP, bank details, addresses, or other private records.
- Downloading, mirroring, indexing, or redistributing leaked databases.
- Building dossiers about private individuals or linking scattered identifiers to identify or locate them.
- Unauthorized access, bypassing access controls, exploiting systems, credential attacks, or evasion.
- Claims that a person or organization committed wrongdoing based only on unverified indicators.

### 5. Intended audience

- Cybersecurity students and early-career practitioners.
- SOC, threat-intelligence, GRC, privacy, and security-awareness teams.
- Organizations developing an initial data inventory or prioritization process.
- Contributors improving defensive documentation and educational resources.

The project is documentation-first and can be used without programming. Suggested artifacts include Markdown guides, CSV templates, diagrams, and synthetic case studies.

### 6. Core deliverables

- A catalog of Indonesia-relevant data categories.
- A proposed Indonesia PII Criticality Ranking (IPCR) model.
- Defensive OSINT and data-exposure checklists.
- Data inventory, evidence-log, and risk-assessment templates.
- Synthetic examples that demonstrate how to document findings without exposing real personal data.

### 7. Important limitations

The IPCR model is a proposed project methodology, not an official Indonesian government classification or a legally binding standard. A score is an aid to decision-making, not an objective statement of legal status or actual harm. Organizations should adapt the framework to their context, consult appropriate professionals, and verify current law and regulatory guidance.

### 8. Guiding principle

**Assess the exposure and its impact; do not collect or publish the person's identity.**
