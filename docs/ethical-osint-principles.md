# Ethical OSINT Principles

## Purpose

Open-source intelligence (OSINT) uses information from sources that are publicly accessible or otherwise lawfully available to an authorized investigator. Public availability does not automatically make every collection, aggregation, republishing, or use appropriate.

This project applies OSINT to defensive security and data-protection objectives. It does not endorse using publicly discoverable information to profile, track, harass, expose, or target private individuals.

## Principles

### 1. Authorization and legitimate purpose

- Define the assessment objective before collecting information.
- Work only within approved scope and applicable law.
- Use a documented authorization process for organizational assessments.
- Stop when the investigation would require access, collection, or processing outside the approved scope.

### 2. Data minimization

- Collect only what is needed to answer the approved security question.
- Prefer recording the data category and exposure status rather than the actual personal value.
- Avoid identifying affected people when a category-level description is sufficient.
- Do not build identity dossiers or combine identifiers to locate or profile private individuals.

### 3. No acquisition or redistribution of leaked records

- Do not download, mirror, index, trade, or publish leaked databases.
- Do not include real NIK, KK, NPWP, bank account numbers, health records, passwords, or other personal secrets in examples.
- If a leak is referenced by a reputable public report, link to the report when appropriate; do not reproduce the underlying records.
- Do not upload sensitive values to public scanning, reputation, or analysis services without explicit approval and a suitable privacy assessment.

### 4. Safe use of public sources

- Prefer official notices, trusted threat-intelligence reports, and approved organizational records.
- Respect website terms, access restrictions, rate limits, and applicable law.
- Do not bypass authentication, paywalls, technical access controls, or access restrictions.
- Do not treat search results, screenshots, usernames, or reputation scores as definitive proof.
- Verify important claims with independent, reliable evidence where possible.

### 5. Protect affected people

- Redact identifiers, personal contact details, account names, faces, QR codes, barcodes, and document numbers from screenshots when they are not necessary.
- Take extra care with children, vulnerable people, health data, biometrics, and financial records.
- Avoid naming alleged victims or suspected wrongdoers in public reports unless there is a compelling, lawful, well-supported reason and appropriate approval.
- Do not encourage contacting, confronting, or publicly exposing individuals based on OSINT findings.

### 6. Confidence and transparency

Use clear evidence labels:

- **Confirmed:** supported by reliable evidence or an authorized verification.
- **Probable:** supported by multiple strong indicators but not fully confirmed.
- **Suspected:** one or more indicators suggest the possibility, with meaningful uncertainty.
- **Unverified:** insufficient evidence to support a conclusion.

Record the source and timestamp, describe limitations, and distinguish observations from interpretation.

### 7. Responsible reporting

When a potential exposure is identified:

1. Preserve minimal, redacted evidence needed to describe the issue.
2. Avoid accessing additional exposed records.
3. Notify the authorized contact, security team, or appropriate official reporting channel.
4. Follow the organization's incident-response process.
5. Do not publish details that would enable other people to exploit the exposure before it is addressed.

### 8. Stop conditions

Stop collection and escalate internally if:

- Authorization or legal basis is unclear.
- The activity encounters restricted or unexpectedly sensitive data.
- The assessment begins to expose identifiable people beyond what is necessary.
- Evidence suggests immediate risk to a person's physical safety, accounts, or finances.
- Continuing would require bypassing controls or acquiring leaked material.

## Ethical review checklist

Before publishing a case study, confirm:

- [ ] The scenario is authorized or entirely synthetic.
- [ ] No real personal identifiers or secrets are included.
- [ ] Evidence is minimal, relevant, and appropriately redacted.
- [ ] Assertions are supported and confidence is stated.
- [ ] The write-up explains protective actions rather than enabling exploitation.
- [ ] The report does not expose affected individuals or provide a path to private records.

**Guiding rule: investigate risks, not private lives.**
